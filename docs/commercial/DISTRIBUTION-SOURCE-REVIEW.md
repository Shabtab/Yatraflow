## Material differences and scope decisions

### Paid expert success remains a separate question

A p. 5 asks whether an unknown expert can earn their first ₹1,000.
Document B distinguishes creator traffic from completed paid expert work on pp. 3 and 5.
B p. 7 recommends a small matched creator-and-expert experiment, with supply growing only after demand appears.

The revised task covers distribution measurement across creators, groups, and travel businesses.
The current six-week pilot uses free plans. It cannot answer the paid expert question.
Its proposed activation gates cannot replace the PDFs' commercial success criteria.

An expert-services follow-up needs a named demand source and a small qualified expert cohort.
It needs a defined offer, scope, delivery window, refund rules, approved payment handling, and actual payout evidence.
Measure qualified inquiries, purchases, fulfillment, satisfaction, refunds, net creator earnings, and time to first ₹1,000.
Manual fulfillment can test the offer before a request inbox or service builder exists.
The current scorecard does not measure those commercial outcomes.
This review neither launches that follow-up nor approves spending, outreach, payouts, or new commercial terms.

### Portfolio and snapshot priorities remain visible

Document B places portfolios and the manual snapshot pilot among its first priorities on pp. 9 and 11–12.
B p. 9 proposes Story, post, and carousel formats. It puts clip automation after distribution evidence.
The current PR repairs measurement and reuses existing public creator pages.
It defers new portfolio maps, media kits, handles, and automated asset generation.

That scope follows the revised measurement task. It keeps the proposed portfolio work visible.
Prepare useful creator pages and manual snapshots within the pilot's stated time budget.
Count voluntary posts and prompted repeats separately. Use the findings to select the next justified tool improvement.
Keep the creator link, route link, licensed assets, and map credits in each manual export.
Do not include proprietary Google content or private planning details in exports.

### Bookmarks are an early signal, not primary activation

Document B includes saves among traveler actions on pp. 2 and 12.
The revised task defines primary activation as a usable persisted create or fork.
Keep local bookmarks separate. The current app has no central bookmark total for this scorecard.

### Partner distribution is distinct from partner booking revenue

Document A discusses eligible partner referrals and completed booking commissions on pp. 1–3 and 5.
The revised Engine C tests whether travel businesses distribute useful plans to existing customers.
These are different hypotheses. A guest-guide fork cannot prove a booking commission, partner sale, or expert payout.
The PDFs alone do not establish a validated hotel or hostel distribution channel.

### Existing commercial and release gates remain open

`STRATEGY.md` retains its earlier creator-only gates and payout prerequisites.
The pilot proposes a smaller, free distribution test. Its thresholds still need explicit ratification.
The existing master tracker, issue #782, also lists security, routing rights, paid-fork, fee, and payout decisions.
Issue #790 owns payout, KYC, reconciliation, and payment-proof requirements.
Free content does not waive security or routing obligations for enabled pilot flows.
These documents do not authorize a fee change or resolve the paid-fork contract.
